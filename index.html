⏱️ ~10 min setup • Replaces physical buttons
▼

A single on-screen button that can do almost anything: Home, Volume, Screenshot, Restart, custom gestures, and more.

  • Apple Support Video (3:40)

Bonus — Priming Siri AI for Your Needs
⏱️ ~5 min • One-time setup
▼

How to give Siri AI the context it needs to help you better — without typing a single settings menu.

Step 1: Create a "My Accessibility Profile" note

Open the Apple Notes app. Create a new note titled exactly:

My Accessibility Profile

In the note, write something like:

I have hand tremors that make precise touch input difficult.
I use Voice Control to navigate my iPhone and iPad hands-free.
I prefer voice commands over tapping when possible.
I use AssistiveTouch as a backup for button replacement.
I wear AirPods Pro and use head gestures to answer calls.
Please suggest voice-first or hands-free solutions when possible.

Step 2: Tell Siri about it

Say: "Siri, remember that I have hand tremors and use Voice Control. Please check my Accessibility Profile note if you're not sure how to help me."

Step 3: Use it

Going forward, when you ask Siri for help with something device-related, it will factor in your preference for hands-free and voice-first solutions. You can also say: "Siri, read my Accessibility Profile note."

💡 Why this works: Siri AI draws from your Notes, Calendar, Messages, and Photos for personal context. A note with a clear title becomes part of your "personal knowledge graph" that Siri references.
